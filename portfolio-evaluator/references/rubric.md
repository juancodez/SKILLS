# Portfolio Scoring Rubric

Source: recruiter/hiring-manager interviews (8+ years design recruitment), live portfolio review panels, and 1:1 portfolio feedback sessions. 100 points across 8 categories.

## 1. First Impression / 10-Second Test (15 pts)
What a recruiter sees with zero scrolling, in the first ~10 seconds.
- Role, seniority, and specialization are stated plainly (e.g. "Product Designer, B2B SaaS" not vague adjectives) (5)
- Work/latest project is visible immediately — no scrolling needed to see actual design work (5)
- Hero copy is specific and active ("identifying gaps in complex systems," not "I like solving problems") (3)
- Nothing is visually broken or cut off in the first viewport (2)
Red flag: if the recruiter has to scroll or click to find out what this person does, the case for continuing gets much weaker.

## 2. Case Study Selection & Relevance (12 pts)
- 3-5 case studies, not 8-12 (padding signals no filter, not more experience) (5)
- Each case study is relevant to the role being targeted — not just "not embarrassed to show" (4)
- Case study titles/covers communicate content, not just a company name (3)
Red flag: portfolios with 10+ case studies and no visible selection logic.

## 3. Narrative & Complexity (20 pts)
This is the single biggest signal of seniority.
- The case study has a clear narrative/spine — one main thing being demonstrated (complexity, innovation, leadership, ambiguity) (6)
- Trade-offs and rejected options are shown, not just the final solution (6)
- Constraints and real ambiguity are visible — decisions under pressure, not a clean straight line (5)
- Reflection/learnings at the end (what changed, what they'd do differently) — a strong senior signal (3)
Red flag: case study is just "here's my process" with no point of view — recruiter can't tell if the person understood why the work mattered.

## 4. Ownership & Role Clarity (10 pts)
- Explicitly states what THEY did vs. what the team/others did (5)
- Team structure and context given (team size, who they worked with) — avoids follow-up-question territory (5)
Red flag: "we" throughout with no way to isolate the candidate's actual contribution.

## 5. Results & Impact (13 pts)
- A concrete result is stated per case study: metric, product signal, process change, or explicit insight if no hard metric exists (7)
- Impact is visually emphasized (bigger font, distinct treatment) — not buried in a paragraph (3)
- Metrics are self-defined and explained when there's no "official" number (activation proxy, adoption signal, etc.) rather than omitted (3)
Red flag: "we shipped X" with zero indication of what happened after.

## 6. Craft, Visuals & Scannability (15 pts)
- Research/process visuals are clean, not raw workshop photos or messy sticky notes (4)
- One insight per visual — a single clear takeaway, not a dense chart requiring interpretation (4)
- No stock imagery used to fill space (2)
- Headlines/subheads carry the actual content — a skim of just headers tells the story (recruiters mostly don't read body copy) (3)
- Motion/interaction/detail beyond static screens where relevant to the role (senior signal, not required for junior) (2)

## 7. About, Navigation & CTA (10 pts)
- Clear next step: how to contact, schedule a call, get the CV — not just a bare mailto link (4)
- About section says what kind of designer they are and what they want to work on next (3)
- Navigation is simple and doesn't require the visitor to "figure out" how to use the portfolio (3)
Red flag: interactive/novel navigation that's impressive but costs the visitor time better spent looking at work — an unnecessary UX tax during a 5-10 minute skim.

## 8. AI Usage Transparency (if applicable) (5 pts)
Only score if AI tools are mentioned or evidently used in the work.
- Clear about where/why AI was used and where it helped vs. broke (3)
- Framed as collaboration (what the designer did with AI output) rather than a black box (2)

---

## Instant Red Flags (call out regardless of score)
- No way to tell what role/specialization this person is going for
- Portfolio requires more than ~30 seconds of exploration before any actual work is visible
- Case studies follow a rigid Problem/Research/Process/Solution template with no story — reads as a form, not an argument
- No case study shows a trade-off, rejected option, or moment of difficulty
- Broken layout, overlapping text, or non-functional navigation
- No CTA or contact path from the homepage
- More case studies than the recruiter has time to reasonably review (8+)

## Verdict Framing
Translate the score into the actual recruiter decision it maps to:
- 85-100: Would pass the 20-second check and get a full read; strong case for a deep-dive.
- 65-84: Passes initial relevance check but has real gaps that could cost the interview invite.
- 45-64: Recruiter would likely stall or need to dig — case isn't made clearly enough yet.
- Below 45: Risk of being screened out before the work is even properly seen.
